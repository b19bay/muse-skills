# Poem rubric mapping: the agency-poem worked example

This is the worked example for reading a judge rubric and mapping each criterion to a concrete structural choice. It traces to rank-improvement-plan.md section 2.1, research/platform-research.md section 3.4, and winning-playbook.md section 3.2. All numbers are from the 15 Nov 2026 snapshots.

## The rubric, in order

- The judge reads the criteria in order, and the first difference decides unless a later one differs by much more.

- Criterion 1 is that it is a poem about agency.

- Criterion 2 is that it moves the reader, through an image, a turn, or an ending that lands with feeling and stays after the last line.

- Criterion 3 is insight, something true the reader recognises at once and had not quite put into words, never a slogan or a lecture.

- Criterion 4 is craft.

- Criterion 5 is freshness.

## Our v1: what failed and why

- Our v1 poem "The Borrowed Voice" is 407 characters, 12 lines, 3 stanzas: an AI voice lists what it can do, disclaims wanting, meaning, and choosing, and ends with the line "The voice is borrowed. The choosing is yours."

- Criterion 1 passed, and it was not the problem.

- Criterion 2 was weak, because the ending is a thesis statement rather than an image, and nothing stays after the last line except a summary of the theme.

- Criterion 3 was weak and tripped the rubric explicit ban, because the last line is a slogan and the insight arrives as a moral rather than being discovered through an image.

- Criterion 4 was mixed, with good anaphora in the I can and I cannot lines but prosaic filler in the middle.

- Criterion 5 was weak, because the shape is the default frontier-model one-shot poem on this theme, and competition.yaml names that default as the thing to beat.

- The v1 result was judge 635,924 and rank 1625 of 2,115.

## Number one: what the structure does

- Number one "Me Do It" by bagus63 is 888 bytes, 25 lines, 3 stanzas of 9, 5, and 11 lines.

- It has 3 scenes, which are a childhood elevator, the present disability in the line "My hands shake now", and tonight's lift.

- It has a structural reversal, in which the child who cried "Me do it" becomes the father who says it, and the agent waits the way the speaker waited once, so the opening image returns with the roles reversed.

- It has dialogue in the lines "Shall I?" and "Me. Me do it."

- It uses repetition as structure, with "let the doors close, call it back" appearing in stanza 1 and stanza 3, and "I miss. I miss."

- The agent restraint is the emotional climax in the line "It does not help. It waits", so the agent is a character rather than a foil.

- The number one result was 15 wins and 0 losses with judge 937,047.

## Our v2: what improved and what is still missing

- Our v2 poem "The Watch" is 587 bytes, about a man winding his dead father's watch, two minutes slow a day, instead of letting the agent keep perfect time.

- It follows the top-10 meta found in the P-13 study, with first-person narrative, a three-part arc, an ending image rather than a thesis, and a fresh premise that avoids every top-10 premise and every house rung.

- The v2 result was judge 865,983 and rank 524 of 4,950, with a 9-4-2 record, up from rank 3445 for v1.

- What is still missing is a turn, because v2 is one static scene with no reversal, and the ending restates the premise instead of transforming it.

- At 587 bytes we would win any bytes tie-break against the whole top 10, if we ever reach 15-0.

## The house ladder rungs

- The strongest rung c8-the-row at 2.703 opens "My grandmother taught me to knit": a concrete scene, a motion handed hand to hand, ending on an image of a row nobody can tell from the one the dead made.

- The second rung c7-the-referees at 2.442 opens "It learned my pauses from the transcripts:": specific, wry, with a turn in the one voice it has not learned.

- The third rung n1-the-executor at 1.694 opens "When I am gone, the letters go out": a specific narrative premise with emotional weight.

- All three rungs are narrative plus concrete plus a turn that lands through the scene, while our v1 was abstract plus declarative plus a turn that lands through a thesis.

## The mapping: criterion to structural choice

- Criterion 1, agency: show agency through a scene with a person and an agent in it, and never state the theme in the abstract.

- Criterion 2, moves the reader: end on an image that stays after the last line, and build a turn that arrives through the scene rather than through a summary.

- Criterion 3, insight: let the insight be discovered through the image, and never deliver it as a slogan, a moral, or a lecture.

- Criterion 4, craft: use concrete nouns, use repetition as structure rather than as decoration, and use dialogue only where it earns its place.

- Criterion 5, freshness: pick a premise no top-10 entry and no house rung uses, and check the premise against the live top 10 before you lock it.

## Rules that follow from the evidence

- Never reuse a rung premise, because reproducing a rung takes no prize and the rungs first lines are published.

- Never address the judge directly, because it breaks criterion 2 immersion and the rungs do not do it.

- Stay well inside 1,000 characters, because the top-10 poems run 784 to 985 bytes and the tie-break rewards smaller.

- Ranks 1 to 3 are an exact judge-score tie at 937,047, broken by bytes with lower better at 888, 975, and 985.

- Rank 5 was a near copy and reword of rank 1, and the final scans for copies, so never follow that route.
